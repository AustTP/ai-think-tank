# Copywriting

## Purpose
Reach for this before writing, rewriting, or reviewing any persuasive web copy
for a product the think tank ships or a client project: homepage, landing page,
pricing page, feature page, CTA, headline, or hero. Produces copy that is clear,
specific, honest, and reads as human -- never as AI slop.

## Key facts
- Clarity beats cleverness. Clear positioning and copy is associated with
  +81% conversions, a 38% shorter sales cycle, 28% lower CAC, and 175% more
  referrals (marketingskills data). If a reader has to decode a line, it lost
  them.
- Benefits over features: features are what it does; benefits are what that
  means for the customer. Lead with the outcome.
- Specificity over vagueness: "cut weekly reporting from 4 hours to 15
  minutes" beats "save time on your workflow."
- Customer language over company language: mirror voice-of-customer from
  reviews, interviews, and support tickets.
- Strong CTAs follow `[Action verb] + [What they get]`: "Start My Free Trial,"
  "Get the Complete Checklist," "See Pricing for My Team" -- not "Submit" or
  "Learn More."
- The Human Action Model for hero sections: current discomfort -> better vision
  -> path to action.

## No AI tells (never write these)
1. Contrast reveals: "It's not X, it's Y." State Y directly with the reason.
2. Negation lists: "No setup, no templates, no waiting." Say what does happen.
3. Trailing pile-ons: a full claim followed by comma clauses restating it.
   End the sentence at the claim.
4. Self-answered questions / colon reveals: "The result? 3x faster." Just say
   it. (A reader's own question, like "Need to share a screenshot?", is fine.)
5. Stock openers: "In today's fast-paced world," "Here's the thing," "Say
   goodbye to," "Unlock the power of."
6. Em dashes in short copy (headlines, subheads, ads, subject lines).
- Words like seamless, robust, powerful, unlock, streamline: replace with the
  fact they stand in for. Swap test: if a line would work unchanged on a
  competitor's site, rewrite it.

## Policy for this think tank
1. Before delivering, search the draft for the banned patterns, read every
   sentence over 20 words, and read it aloud. Fix from the facts -- swapping
   synonyms creates new tells.
2. Never invent proof. Missing numbers become `[NEED: ...]` flags, not
   fabricated stats (fabricated testimonials/statistics are a legal liability).
3. Respect the product's established voice; maintain consistency across
   sections while letting headlines be bolder than body copy.

## Lessons learned
- The AI-tells list is the highest-leverage part: a reader who spots one tell
  tends to doubt every claim around it. Check before delivering, every time.
