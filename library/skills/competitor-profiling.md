# Competitor Profiling

## Purpose
Reach for this before any competitor research or "who's in this space" question
for a product, project, or sales target. Produces structured, evidence-based
profiles from real public sources -- a competitor's own website, docs, pricing
page, reviews, and press -- not vibes or hearsay.

## Key facts
- A profile answers five columns for every competitor: what they sell, how they
  position it, what it costs, who it's for, and how they talk about it.
- Source the profile from URLs (competitor homepage, /pricing, /features,
  /about, docs, changelog, reviews, funding announcements) using the api_call /
  browse tools. Every claim carries its source.
- The "search term clues" method: use known search phrases about the space to
  find who actually shows up, and mine one known competitor's pages for the
  keywords they target, then search those keywords to map the field.
- Keep a living competitor matrix (one row per competitor, same columns) so
  positioning gaps show up as empty cells -- those empty cells are the
  differentiators.
- Positioning statement template: "For [target], [product] is a [category] that
  [primary benefit] instead of [primary alternative] because [key
  differentiation]."
- Review mining: read reviews of competitors to find unmet needs and to steal
  their vocabulary (customers describe the problem in their own words).

## Policy for this think tank
1. No profile without a URL source; mark anything inferred as `[inferred]`.
2. Profiles stay neutral and factual -- never trash-talk a competitor, just
  record their actual offering and pricing.
3. End every profile with the 2-3 open gaps or differentiators it surfaced,
  phrased as questions the team can act on.
4. Keep the matrix current as a project artifact the team can update.

## Lessons learned
- The value is in the columns, not the prose: a uniform matrix surfaces gaps
  that a free-form summary hides. Update it as the space moves.
