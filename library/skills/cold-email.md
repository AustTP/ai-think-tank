# Cold Email

## Purpose
Reach for this before drafting any outbound cold email for a product, a
partnership, or a sale. Produces short, peer-voiced, deliverable-friendly B2B
cold emails that get replies -- not spam.

## Key facts
- Short wins. 100-150 words max for a first touch; most gets cut, so lead with
  the one relevant reason.
- Write to a peer, not a lead: specific, informed, respectful. The voice is
  what separates a cold email from spam.
- Personalization is required, not optional: reference something real about the
  recipient (their product, a recent post, an obvious overlap). One strong
  specific detail beats a paragraph of vague flattery.
- Structure: a relevant subject line, one-line context (why them, why now),
  a clear ask that is easy to say yes to, and a graceful off-ramp.
- Deliverability matters: use a real domain, warm up, keep volume reasonable,
  avoid spam-trigger words and excessive links, respect the recipient's time.
- Stop everything on reply: a response means the conversation moved to
  email/call; never keep auto-sequencing after a human replies.

## Policy for this think tank
1. Draft stays under ~150 words; anything longer gets cut, not padded.
2. No fake urgency, no invented mutual contacts, no claiming an introduction
  that doesn't exist.
3. The ask must be specific and replyable (e.g. "is that a problem you
  have?" / "15-minute call to see if it's relevant?").
4. Never send without a personalized, verifiable detail about the recipient.
5. On reply: stop the sequence, hand off to the human, note the outcome in the
  project log.

## Lessons learned
- The reply is the only metric that matters. Everything that reduces a reply
  (length, vagueness, fake urgency) is the thing to cut first.
