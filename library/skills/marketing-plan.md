# Marketing Plan

## Purpose
Reach for this before drafting any go-to-market / growth / marketing plan for a
product the think tank ships, or for a client project. Produces an executable
12-month plan structured by AARRR (Acquisition, Activation, Retention,
Referral, Revenue) and grounded in the product's real stage, budget, and team
capacity -- not a generic template.

## Key facts
- AARRR forces every recommendation to be funnel-stage-tagged, which makes the
  plan executable in priority order: Acquisition (strangers -> aware),
  Activation (aware -> first valued experience), Retention (repeat users),
  Referral (users -> more users), Revenue (monetization).
- Marketing is compounding, not a campaign calendar. Treat the plan as a
  portfolio of buy-and-hold assets (SEO content, a newsletter, a community, a
  referral loop) over one-off spikes. No single channel carries the plan; the
  win is many small compounding assets, not one silver bullet.
- "One asset, many returns": prefer assets with the most downstream reuse (a
  cornerstone piece ranks in search, earns backlinks, feeds a newsletter, seeds
  social). Sequence moves around reuse.
- "Audition, not an auction": earn attention by being worth paying attention
  to, not by buying a captive audience. Marketing is non-deterministic -- run a
  portfolio of bets and double down on what works.
- Market-quality gate: score the core problem on size (pain/urgency) and
  frequency. Big + frequent is the best quadrant to build in; weak quadrants
  constrain realistic CAC and budget math. State the quadrant plainly.
- Budget: revenue-based (5-40% of ARR) or goal-based
  `[(New ARR / (ARPC x 12)) x CAC] / annual retention rate`, plus 10-20%
  experimental budget on top. CAC unknown is the highest-impact open decision.
- Real SaaS growth is S-curves with plateaus, not hockey sticks. Budget
  70/20/10 across current / next / experimental initiatives so the next
  S-curve is funded before the current one plateaus.
- Stage tiers: pre-seed $0-2K/mo organic only; seed $5-15K/mo paid test;
  deployment $20-50K/mo; Series A $50-150K/mo; B+ $150K+/mo. Anchor to these,
  adjust for category.

## Policy for this think tank
1. Every plan section names the AARRR stage it serves; the 90-day roadmap has
   owners and kill criteria, not just actions.
2. Ground in real numbers (the product's actual budget, ARPC, retention,
   channels) and label anything unconfirmed as an open decision -- never gloss
   over churn or an unknown CAC.
3. The exec summary must stand alone (readable in 60 seconds); the rest should
   reward deep reading. No marketing-speak, no invented stats.
4. A generic plan is a failed plan: name what the team can actually execute at
   current size and what unlocks when funding closes.

## Lessons learned
- The biggest failure mode is a plan that lists tactics without sequencing,
  recommends what the team can't execute, or pretends paid budget exists before
  it does. Every plan section should answer "who, when, what metric."
