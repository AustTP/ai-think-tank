# Marketing Psychology

## Purpose
Reach for this before writing copy, designing a landing page, or planning an
acquisition channel, when you want the message to actually move people instead
of just describe a product. It is a toolkit of mental models (buyer psychology)
to apply consciously to marketing and sales content.

## Key facts
- Apply one or a few mental models per piece, chosen deliberately for the
  audience and stage -- not all of them at once.
- Core models:
  - Problem Agitation Solution (PAS): surface the problem, agitate the pain,
    offer the solution. Copywriting workhorse.
  - Before/After Bridge (BAB): show the world before, the world after, and how
    to cross. Good for transformation narratives.
  - Social Proof: use numbers ("10,000 teams"), not hype. Specific > vague.
  - Authority / Expert Status: cite credentials and results; earn trust.
  - Scarcity & Urgency: honest deadlines and limited availability, never fake
    pressure.
  - Loss Aversion: people work harder to avoid losing than to gain ("don't
    lose your spot" vs "join now").
  - Curiosity Gap: open a question and answer it (without resorting to
    clickbait).
  - Anchoring: show the expensive option first so the intended one looks
    reasonable.
  - Reciprocity: give genuine value first (a free tool, guide, or insight).
  - Commitment/Consistency: small yeses first; low-friction first step.
  - IKEA Effect / Ownership: let users build or customize; increases perceived
    value.
  - Halo Effect: a strong single impression colors everything else; get the
    headline and first impression right.
- Sequence beats a pile: craft a narrative arc (attention -> interest ->
  desire -> action, AIDA) rather than stacking every model in one paragraph.

## Policy for this think tank
1. Choose models deliberately and note which ones you applied and why -- a
  flagged-model comment beats an unlabelled mix.
2. No manipulative or fake scarcity, no manufactured urgency, no dark
  patterns. These models lower friction and raise clarity; they never deceive.
3. Match the model to the stage: acquisition copy leans on PAS/BAB and
  curiosity; retention copy leans on reciprocity and consistency.

## Lessons learned
- A model is only as good as its fit: stacking five models into one paragraph
  reads as manipulative. Apply one to a page and you'll feel the difference.
